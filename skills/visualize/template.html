A question is words.
A document is numbers.
Ranking is what happens between.

Every search across this knowledge base runs the same two-act machine. Act one happens ahead of time, turning each document into three weighted columns of text. Act two happens per question, turning a sentence into terms, scoring every document that shares one, then re-ordering the result by a rule that scoring alone cannot express. Every number below was traced out of this project's live index.

The system at a glance

Two pictures before the detail: where documents come from and what gets built from them, then the path a single question takes through what was built.

Act one

Building the index

Runs when documents change, not when you search. Its whole job is deciding what words each document can later be found by — which is why how you write a document is a retrieval decision, not only a style one.

01

Take the document apart

Frontmatter, title and body are separated and stored as different things. The title is not merely the first line of the body — it becomes its own column, and it will be weighted heaviest.

Relations bullets are stripped before indexing. A cross-reference list would make every document searchable by its neighbours' topics.

one real document, taken apart
02

Break every identifier apart

Code names get searched in more ways than they get written. Each identifier is split into every form somebody might type: whole, punctuation collapsed, each segment, and each camel-case part.

Function words falling out of a split are dropped — the And of RepairAndMigrate would otherwise match nearly every document in the corpus.

03

Write three columns, and two tables nothing searches

Only three columns are searchable, and they carry different weight. The idents column is the catch-all for everything that should be findable but does not belong in prose: expanded identifiers, tags, declared names, and aliases.

Two more tables sit deliberately outside search. Evidence paths would add noise, and bulk symbol records would swamp the word statistics that make ranking work at all.

Act two

Answering a question

Runs per search. Pick either question and every panel below fills with what this index actually returns for it. Both are real questions against this corpus.

04

Turn the sentence into terms

The question is lowercased, split, and stripped of function words. Identifiers in the question get the same expansion documents got in step 02.

What survives is joined with OR, so a document matches if it contains any term. Matching is generous on purpose; the narrowing happens in scoring.

05

Score every match, weighted by column

BM25 rewards a document for containing a rare term and penalises it for length. A word appearing in thousands of documents is worth almost nothing — which is exactly why a tag applied in bulk quietly destroys its own searchability.

Scores come out negative, and more negative is better. The bars run right to left, so longer means a better match.

curated fact reference entry declares your term
06

Re-rank with field signals and declared names

A reference entry gets unless it declares one of your terms. Field coverage and ordered word pairs can improve its score. A whole-name lookup gives declaring documents priority. Superseded documents remain last.

What this means when you write one

Four consequences that follow directly from the machine above.

Title

Write the claim, not a label

Aliases

Add the words you did not use

Search matches words, not meanings. A document explaining that a setting "scales the time" cannot be reached by "slow down game time" — nothing is shared. Aliases supply the missing words.

Idents

Declare only what you own

A declared name waives the penalty for questions naming it. Declaring names a document merely mentions is how it comes to outrank the true answer for a name it does not own.

Repetition

A phrase in thousands of docs is worth nothing

Document frequency is the whole basis of scoring. A tag applied in bulk across a family of documents destroys its own search weight and drags unrelated questions down with it.
